# ShiftMate product facts for editorial use

Use this file as the default factual boundary for AI-generated Blogger content. If a new brief explicitly provides a newer fact, the brief may override this file for that item.

## Product identity

- Product name: **ShiftMate**
- Global store positioning: **ShiftMate – Nurse Shift Calendar**
- Korean legacy brand: **나는간호사다**
- Primary audience: nurses and hospital/healthcare shift workers
- Broader brand direction: useful for people who work rotating or irregular shifts

## Core capabilities

ShiftMate supports core workflows such as:

- shift schedule / duty calendar management
- personal schedule/calendar management
- work alarms and reminders
- coworker shift sharing and viewing
- home-screen widgets
- memo/check memo workflows
- shift/work analysis and statistics
- customization and display settings

Do not imply that every feature is identical on every platform or locale unless the brief explicitly confirms it.

## Multi-device shift sync (10.0 release copy)

- ShiftMate 10.0 release notes describe shift synchronization across multiple devices with the same account.
- Phrase this as synchronization of **supported shift data**, not automatic synchronization of all phone calendars, alarms, permissions, or device-local settings.
- Device and data-type behavior may differ. Do not promise identical features on iOS/Android or claim every datum syncs instantly.

## Coworker shift freshness

Current safe wording:

- Coworker shift data is fetched from the server when the coworker-shift screen is entered and when the displayed month changes.
- Do **not** describe background real-time push reflection as released unless the brief explicitly says that release is complete.

## Editorial claim rules

Unless the brief provides verified supporting information, do not claim:

- download counts, active-user counts, rankings, awards, or market leadership
- clinical outcomes, sleep/health benefits, reduced errors, or safety improvements
- specific hospital adoption or endorsements
- a feature is available when it is only planned
- exact release dates, version numbers, or platform parity

When writing about app operation, prefer concrete descriptions of what the user can do over promotional adjectives.
